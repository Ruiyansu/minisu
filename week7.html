After-class Reflection Log: Artificial Intelligence and Human Values

1. Before class: Watched the short film "We Were Here"

Before class, I watched the short film "We Were Here". This film made me think about whether AI will replace human jobs in the future. It is undeniable that AI does work faster in organizing tasks, and there are many models available now. However, I believe it cannot completely replace humans because humans have emotions, feelings, creativity, and personal experiences. These are the important differences between humans and AI.

2. Class Activity 1: Social Media Data and Personal Identity

In the previous class, we observed 32 social media accounts, including 16 friends and 16 bloggers. We classified their posts into different categories and compared the results. I noticed that "Lifestyle" was a relatively common category in my friends' posts. However, I think this category is not detailed enough to reflect the differences among individuals. For example, some of my friends post videos of their daily lives, while others mainly share their personal photos. Although they are all classified in the same category, their content and sharing methods are quite different. This made me realize that the classification of social media may be too general and cannot truly understand a person's identity. We can learn about a person's interests through online data, but we cannot fully understand their real life and personality just by their posted content. Moreover, the accuracy of AI's algorithms depends entirely on the information given by humans, and it has certain biases, just like the father in the film feeding the AI with incorrect pizza words.

3. Class Activity 2: The Reliability of AI and Academic Evaluation

Another interesting discussion was about the reliability of AI. In the video, we saw someone deliberately providing incorrect information to the AI, but the AI treated these as facts. This made me realize that AI is not always reliable, and we should not completely trust every answer it gives. The teacher in class also reminded us not to upload our research results to AI for checking, which also relates to the security and ownership of original works. After uploading ideas and research results, is it possible to store or reuse them? After that, we did a secret task called "Plan B", where we needed to pretend to be AI systems and determine whether the article in front of us was written by AI or humans. I think this activity was very interesting because it made me realize that accurately determining whether an article is generated by AI or humans is not easy. Since this was a secret task, I won't go into detail about the content. We also discussed what makes AI seem human-like and what the differences are between humans and AI. I believe personal experience, emotions, and original ideas are important characteristics of humans. AI systems may judge an article based on its language and structure, but they may not truly understand the author's thoughts and creativity.

4. Overall Reflection

Overall, today's class made me think more deeply about the relationship between AI and humans. AI can replace some of human work, analyze social media data, and even evaluate students' homework, but it cannot fully understand the true identity, emotions, and creative ideas of a person. I think AI is a useful tool, but we should not rely entirely on it. Our own judgment, personal experience, and creativity are still very important.
