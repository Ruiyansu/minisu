Week 2 Notes

Core Topic: From Networks to Platform Society

This week we talked about how the internet has changed over time. In the early days, the internet was more open and decentralised. It was like a big network where different websites were connected together, and people had more freedom to create and share content. But as time went on, the internet gradually moved into the platform society. Now most of our online activities happen inside large company platforms, such as Instagram, TikTok and YouTube. These platforms are not just simple websites. They are designed to connect users, collect our personal data, and finally turn this data into business profit. In this kind of society, a lot of our daily social life, communication and even entertainment are controlled by these big platforms.

Key Concept: Algorithms and User Resistance

We also learned about algorithms. An algorithm is basically a set of rules written by human beings. A very important point is that most platform algorithms are private, which means ordinary users cannot see exactly how they work. That is why we often call it a black box. A good example is TikTok. It watches what videos we like, watch repeatedly or skip quickly, and then keeps recommending similar content to us. This creates the so-called filter bubble. Sometimes the algorithm will also suppress or reduce the visibility of certain content. In response, users, especially activists, will use algospeak. They change their wording or use emojis to avoid being restricted by the platform. From this lesson, I started to realise that algorithmic problems are not only a technical issue. Social power, company interests and human choices are all involved.

Workshop Reflection

In the workshop, we were introduced to the task of building our own portfolio website. The teacher told us there were two main choices: we could use ready-made website building tools, or we could write code by ourselves. I chose to learn coding because I really wanted to try this way and understand how a website is built from the very beginning. However, up to now, I have found this process much harder than I expected. There are so many codes and rules that I cannot fully remember. When I meet errors or layout problems, I still need to look for extra help. Even so, I think this experience is still meaningful, because I am slowly getting familiar with HTML and CSS.
